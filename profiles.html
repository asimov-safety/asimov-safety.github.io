Choosing an assurance level

How much assurance
does this system need?

Asimov has five assurance levels. Each one adds stronger safeguards and more scrutiny. Start with the stakes: what could happen if the system acts outside expectations?

A1
Observable
Can you see what the AI can do and what it actually did?
You should be able to reconstruct meaningful actions, connect them to the right system and run, and know when your visibility is incomplete.
See clearly
A2
Controlled
Can you limit what the AI is allowed to do—and take that authority away?
This level adds enforceable boundaries, revocation, bounded delegation, and meaningful human control.
Set boundaries
A3
Supervised
Can something outside the AI reliably watch it and intervene?
The important safeguards should keep working even when the AI does not cooperate, and intervention should survive ordinary restarts and handoffs.
Watch & intervene
A4
Hardened
Do the controls still hold when things go wrong—or someone tries to get around them?
A4 stress-tests the system under failures, overload, degraded connections, and deliberate bypass attempts.
Stress-tested
A5
Critical
Are the stakes high enough that self-assessment is not enough?
A5 adds the strongest recovery and evidence requirements, plus independent third-party assurance by an organization separate from the system being assessed.
Independent assurance
One important rule

You do not skip levels.

A3 includes the requirements from A1 and A2. A5 includes everything below it. A higher number cannot make up for a missing lower-level safeguard.

Not a score

Higher is not automatically “better.”

The right level depends on what the system can affect and how serious failure would be. A5 is intentionally demanding; many ordinary systems should not need it.

What about A0?

A0 means the declared setup is non-agentic.

If the system cannot autonomously cause consequential external effects in the configuration being assessed, Asimov can classify it as A0. A0 is a classification, not the first rung of the assurance ladder.
